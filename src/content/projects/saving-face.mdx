## Overview

Over winter break 2021 I joined MIT Media Lab as a student researcher on
*Saving Face* — a suite of easily scaled technologies to help people fight
the pandemic by warning them when they're about to touch their faces. Small
team: three researchers and one engineer; I carried the research and visual
design side from January to May.

## The approach

The mobile app takes a SONAR-inspired approach: your earbuds transmit an
ultrasound signal, and the microphone — positioned near your face — receives
it. From that echo the app measures the distance between hand and face, and
sounds an alarm when they get too close. No camera, no wearable, nothing to
buy: just the earbuds already in your pocket.

## What I did

My job was making the technology legible to people who'd never think about
ultrasound:

- Designed and refined the mobile app's UI.
- Created the user manual and the visual materials around the system.
- Worked with the product researchers on the justification documentation —
  the *why this works* alongside the *how to use it*.
- Integrated the written instructions, graphics, and video content for the
  project's online publication, including the video walkthrough.

## What it taught me

Saving Face was my first time designing for a system whose core interaction
is invisible — you can't see ultrasound, so the interface and the guidance
materials *are* the product's legibility. Explaining an unseen mechanism
clearly enough that people trust an alarm from it turned out to be a design
problem in itself, and it's the same problem I've been working on ever since:
making invisible systems read clearly.
