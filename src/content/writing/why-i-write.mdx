Designing and writing have always felt like two hands doing the same work.
One shapes. The other names.

Most of my favorite projects didn't start in Figma. They started in a
notebook — a paragraph about a person I'd interviewed, a sentence I couldn't
let go of, a question I kept returning to. By the time I opened a design
file, I already knew what I was looking for. The frames just gave it a body.

I don't think this is unusual. Most designers I admire write — even if they
don't call it that. A good research synthesis is writing. A good case study
is writing. A crit, if it lands, is writing out loud.

What writing gives me that design sometimes doesn't is the pressure to be
honest about what I *actually* think, versus what would look coherent on a
slide. You can hide a lot behind a nice grid. It's harder to hide behind a
sentence.

So I keep both practices going, and let them talk to each other.
