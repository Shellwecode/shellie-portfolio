A quiet interface isn't the same as a minimal one. Minimal is a visual
decision — fewer elements, more whitespace. Quiet is a *behavioral* decision
— fewer interruptions, more trust in the person using the thing.

A minimal app can still be loud. It can ping you, prompt you, pop a modal,
ask for five-star ratings. The elegance of the shell doesn't fix the noise
inside.

A quiet app assumes you know why you opened it, and tries not to change your
mind. It surfaces what you asked for. It waits. It remembers where you were.
It doesn't celebrate itself.

Most of the work in making something quiet happens in the invisible places:

— The default state of a form.
— The moment after an action completes.
— The empty state when there's nothing yet to show.
— The second time you open it.

These are the places products usually try hardest to *help*. I think they're
the places we should try hardest to get out of the way.
